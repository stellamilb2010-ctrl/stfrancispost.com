Are You Actually Tired, or Just Mentally Overloaded?

By Maria Eduarda Simoes · October 7, 2026

Have you ever come home from school feeling completely exhausted, even though you basically spent the whole day sitting down?

You get home thinking you’ll start your homework, maybe be productive for once, but then you lie down for “five minutes” and suddenly doing anything feels impossible. And the weird thing is, sometimes you’re not even sleepy. You’re just… tired.

That might be because being tired isn’t always physical. Sometimes, your brain just has too much going on.

Think about a normal school day. You’re moving between completely different subjects every hour, thinking about deadlines, tests, homework, sports, plans with friends, and probably something you forgot to do. At the same time, your phone is constantly there. Messages, TikToks, Instagram, notifications. Even when we get a break, we normally fill it with something else.

So when does your brain actually get a break?

The “I can multitask” lie

I think most of us are guilty of this.

You’re doing homework on your laptop, your phone is next to you, music is playing, and every few minutes you stop to answer someone. Then you go back to your assignment, remember you need to do something for another class, open another tab… and somehow you now have 15 tabs open and have barely finished anything. It feels like multitasking, but our brains aren’t actually that good at constantly switching between things. Research from the American Psychological Association explains that changing between tasks can slow us down and make it harder to concentrate. Basically, every time you stop writing your essay to check a notification, your brain has to switch its attention and then switch back again.

Do that all day, and it makes sense that you’re tired.

Is scrolling actually resting?

This one hurts a little.

After school, one of the easiest things to do is lie in bed and scroll. And obviously there’s nothing wrong with watching TikTok or going on your phone. But I started thinking about whether that actually counts as a proper break. You might not be doing schoolwork, but your brain is still taking in something new every few seconds. A video, a song, a comment, a message, another video. A CDC study found that around half of American teenagers reported spending four or more hours a day on screens outside of schoolwork. Higher screen time was also linked with being less likely to feel well-rested. That doesn’t mean your phone is automatically the reason you’re tired, but it does make you think about how much information we take in every day. Sometimes going for a walk, exercising, showering, listening to music, or even just doing absolutely nothing for a few minutes might be more of a break than we realize.

Or maybe… you just need to sleep

Of course, not everything is “mental overload.” Sometimes you are tired because you stayed up way too late.

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends that teenagers get around 8–10 hours of sleep each night. Which sounds great until you remember homework exists. Sleep affects concentration, memory, and learning, so no productivity trick will completely replace it. But the important part is realizing you can be physically tired, mentally tired, or both.

So what can we actually do?

Probably not wake up at 5 a.m., journal, meditate, run 10 km, and drink a green juice before school.

It can be much simpler than that.

Write things down instead of trying to remember every deadline in your head. Put your phone away for a while when you really need to focus. Take an actual break without feeling guilty about it. And maybe accept that you don’t need to be productive every second of the day.

We’re constantly being told how to manage our time better, study more efficiently, and fit more into our days. But maybe sometimes we don’t need to fit more in. Maybe we need to have a little less going on.

So next time you come home from school wondering why you’re exhausted even though you “did nothing all day,” remember that sitting down doesn’t mean your brain wasn’t busy. Sometimes, it’s not that you need to try harder. Your brain might just need a break.
